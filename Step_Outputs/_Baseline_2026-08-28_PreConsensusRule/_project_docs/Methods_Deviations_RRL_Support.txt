WHAT CHANGED FROM THE PROPOSAL — AND WHY (SIMPLE VERSION)
============================================================
23 real changes to the methods. Each one: what changed, why, and if
there's a real published paper backing it up. All links checked live
today — open them yourself before citing, don't just trust this file.

1. TOXICITY CHECK (ToxinPred)
------------------------------
CHANGED: A peptide only gets marked "toxic" if there's real proof
(matches a known toxin, or has a toxin-like motif) — not just a raw
score.
WHY: The raw score alone called human albumin, human GAPDH, a plain
linker, AND our own vaccine adjuvant all "toxic." That's clearly wrong.
Zero of our 1,025 real peptides had any actual toxin match.
BACKED BY: Yes. The tool's own paper (Sharma et al. 2022 — already in
your references) shows the "real proof" method beats the raw-score
method. https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbac174

2. STABILITY SCORE (Instability Index)
----------------------------------------
CHANGED: A shaky stability score on the source protein no longer
throws out its epitopes. It's just noted, not used to reject.
WHY: All 27 Mpox A35R samples scored 41.03 — just barely over the
40 cutoff — and this was silently deleting an entire required antigen.
The short pieces we actually use get their own real stability check
later anyway (Phase 2A).
BACKED BY: No direct paper found. This is our own judgment call —
say so if asked, don't claim it's "standard practice."

3. TOP-10 PER GROUP (before manual allergy check)
---------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: New step — pick only the best 10 candidates per antigen per
type before sending to manual allergy screening. (Title corrected from
an earlier "TOP-5" — the code has used TOP_N_PER_TARGET_PER_CLASS = 10
throughout; only this write-up's title was stale.)
WHY: Fixing #1 above increased candidates from 108 to ~1,290 — way
too many to check by hand.
BACKED BY: Partially. A real TB-vaccine paper (Mistry & Flower 2017)
does the same kind of prioritizing. But that same paper also warns
that picking by ONE score alone is too narrow — which is why we rank
by two things (conservancy + binding strength), not one.
https://doi.org/10.6026/97320630013220

4. MPOX CLADE LABELING
------------------------
CHANGED: Instead of requiring "Clade IIa/IIb" in the record (which
returned ZERO B5R and A35R sequences), we now tag each sequence
"confirmed" or "unconfirmed" clade and keep both.
WHY: NCBI genuinely has no clade info on file for B5R/A35R sequences
— confirmed by checking all of them. Not a bug, a real data gap.
BACKED BY: Yes. A real NCBI paper (Schäffer et al. 2020) documents
that viral sequence records are often incompletely labeled.
https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-020-3537-3

5. TRAINING DATA SIZE (ESM-2 model)
--------------------------------------
CHANGED: Used 774 real sequences (390 antigenic + 384 non-antigenic)
instead of the planned 500 + 500.
WHY: That's what we could actually gather for real — no fake data.
BACKED BY: Yes, generally. A 2025 paper shows smaller real datasets
still work fine for this kind of model. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-05674-x
Our own test result (98.6% accuracy score on data the model never saw)
is the stronger proof it worked, though.

6. ALLERGENFP TOOL — SHORT PEPTIDES
--------------------------------------
CHANGED: Peptides shorter than 16 letters skip AllergenFP (that tool
can't process them) and use the other two checks instead.
WHY: Confirmed directly on the real tool — it rejected a 9-letter
peptide that the other tool accepted fine.
BACKED BY: Partially. The original AllergenFP paper explains why the
tool needs a minimum length, but doesn't state "16" specifically —
that exact number is our own finding, not from their paper.

7. ALLELE FREQUENCIES — RESOLVED (was placeholder, now real data)
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: The paper says allele-frequency tables were "downloaded from
IEDB." They now come from AFND (allelefrequencies.net), population
"Singapore Riau Malay" (pop_id=2042, n=132), retrieved 2026-08-23.
107 alleles across all six loci (A=22, B=33, C=19, DRB1=18, DQB1=7,
DPB1=8), 2-field, filtered to those MHCflurry/MHCnuggets support.
Earlier session versions used reconstructed estimates; those are gone.

WHY NOT PHILIPPINE DATA (this is the part to disclose):
AFND has no usable Philippine table for this. Verified directly:
  - Philippines National Capital Region (n=51) — Metro Manila, exactly
    the paper's target — is 1-FIELD ONLY (A*02, not A*02:01). Unusable.
  - The only 2-field Philippine data is "Philippines Ivatan" (n=50), an
    indigenous Batanes population, not representative of lowland
    Filipinos.
  - HLA-DQB1 and HLA-DPB1 have NO Philippine data at any resolution —
    and those loci carry most of the MHC-II coverage.
Singapore Riau Malay was chosen as the closest usable proxy: Austronesian,
same lineage as lowland Filipinos, complete 2-field data on all six loci.

HOW TO DESCRIBE IT: "Southeast Asian (Austronesian) proxy population
coverage," NOT "Philippine population coverage." The paper's "downloaded
from IEDB" wording must be amended to cite AFND (Gonzalez-Galarza et al.,
Nucleic Acids Research 2020, 48:D783-8).

BACKED BY: AFND is a real, citable, peer-reviewed database. The proxy
substitution itself is our documented choice, forced by data absence.

8. MHC-II ALLELE PANEL WIDENED (5 -> 21)
-------------------------------------------
CHANGED: MHC-II binding was originally screened against 5 DRB1 alleles
only. Now screens 21 across three loci (9 DRB1 + 6 DQ + 6 DP).
WHY: Phase 1F scores coverage across DRB1+DQ+DP, but Phase 1D only ever
tested DRB1 — so 11 of 21 alleles could never appear as "bound" no matter
how well a peptide actually bound them, including HLA-DPB1*05:01 (the
highest-frequency allele in the whole MHC-II panel). This capped
cumulative MHC-II coverage at ~84% for structural reasons, not biological
ones.
BACKED BY: The paper's own coverage formula combines "loci" (plural), so
screening all three MHC-II loci is MORE faithful to the stated method,
not less. Note honestly that a wider panel also makes the 90% threshold
easier to reach — but the fix was made because the old panel was
internally inconsistent with Phase 1F, not to pass the gate.

9. 90% POPULATION COVERAGE — PRIORITIZED, NOT REQUIRED
---------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Peptides below 90% coverage are no longer deleted. They're
kept, flagged BELOW_TARGET, and ranked below the >=90% ones.
WHY: The paper says peptides at >=90% were "PRIORITIZED as
population-covering candidates" — prioritized, not required. The code
was deleting them. Same wording issue already corrected for GRAVY
("deprioritized") and Surface_Charge ("deprioritizes").
WHY IT MATTERED: Measured cumulative MHC-II coverage tops out below 90%,
so the hard filter produced a construct with ZERO helper-T-cell epitopes
— no GPGPG linkers, no CD4+ help, and Phase III's helper-T-cell research
questions unanswerable.
BACKED BY: The paper's own wording. But this IS a real interpretive
choice — disclose it.

10. FOLDX OUTPUT — RENAMED FROM "ΔΔG" TO TOTAL FOLDING ENERGY
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Step 2C still runs FoldX's `--command=Stability`, but the
result is now reported and labeled as FoldX_Total_Energy_kcal_mol, not
ΔΔG. The paper's "ΔΔG > +1.5 kcal/mol" REVIEW trigger has been removed
from the decision logic; the value is kept as contextual only.
WHY: ΔΔG is, by definition, a difference between a mutant and a
wild-type structure. This pipeline has no designed mutants — it screens
one non-mutational multi-epitope construct — so there is no wild-type
counterpart to difference against. `--command=Stability` was always
just the raw total folding energy of the one structure given to it;
calling that "ΔΔG" and applying a ΔΔG-shaped threshold to it was a
mislabeling, not a computation error.
WHY IT MATTERED: A ΔΔG gate applied to a quantity that isn't a ΔΔG can
reject (or pass) a candidate for a reason that doesn't actually mean
what the report says it means.
BACKED BY: This is a terminology/applicability correction, not a claim
requiring a citation — the paper's own ΔΔG criterion is simply N/A to a
non-mutational workflow, and the write-up should say so.

11. PHENIX REFINEMENT — MOVED TO STEP 2C, NOT STEP D
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Phenix geometry refinement (`phenix.geometry_minimization`,
optionally `phenix.model_idealization`) now runs inside Step 2C's
structure-prep chain (PDBFixer -> OpenMM -> Phenix), before SASA,
FoldX, APBS, and the manual Aggrescan3D/CamSol/DeepSol submissions —
not as a separate pass after Step 2D's MolProbity validation.
WHY: Running Phenix after Step C meant every solubility/aggregation/
stability metric in Step C described an earlier, geometrically rougher
structure than the one MolProbity ultimately validated in Step D — two
different structures being reported as one candidate's results.
Refining before Step C's other analyses means every number downstream
(SASA, FoldX, the manual patch-location submissions, and Step D's
MolProbity numbers) describes the SAME final structure.
BACKED BY: This is a pipeline-ordering fix, not a scientific
substitution — the paper specifies Phenix refinement without pinning
it to a particular step boundary; running it earlier is a deviation
from the paper's implied step order, disclosed here for that reason.

12. JUNCTION CYSTEINE RULE vs THE ADJUVANT (Phase 2A)
-----------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: The ">2 cysteines in a junction peptide = reject" rule is now
applied only to EPITOPE-EPITOPE junctions. Junctions that reach back into
the beta-defensin-3 adjuvant are exempted and reported as a flag.
WHY: The paper requires beta-defensin-3 as the adjuvant AND requires this
cysteine rule. They contradict each other. Mature human beta-defensin-3
natively has SIX cysteines forming three disulfide bonds -- that ladder is
what makes it a defensin. Measured on our construct: 116 junction peptides
break the limit, and ALL of them trace to just 3 sites (KK@43, EAAAK@45,
KK@49) that overlap the adjuvant's cysteines at positions 32/39/40. All 34
epitope-epitope junctions are clean. Applied literally the rule rejects any
construct using the adjuvant the paper mandates -- it can never be passed.
AFTER THE FIX: 4 junctions exempted, 33 checked, 0 violations -> passes on
merit rather than being blocked by the adjuvant's own biology.
BACKED BY: The rule's stated purpose is catching toxin-like cysteine-rich
peptides created incidentally at NOVEL boundaries. The adjuvant is a known,
characterised, deliberately included molecule whose disulfides are
functionally essential -- not an unintended junction risk. Still our own
interpretive call: disclose it.

13. JUNCTION TOXICITY -- EVIDENCE RULE EXTENDED FROM PHASE 1 TO PHASE 2A
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Phase 2A's junction-peptide toxicity check now uses ToxinPred2's
hybrid model (-m 2) and flags a junction TOXIC only on real toxin evidence
-- a BLAST homology hit or a MERCI motif hit -- exactly as Phase 1E already
does (see #1). The composition-only ML score is still computed and reported
as an advisory COMPOSITION-FLAG, but no longer rejects on its own.
WHY: Phase 2A was still running the composition-only model (-m 1) and
thresholding its raw score -- the exact miscalibrated path #1 had already
rejected for Phase 1. Measured effect on this construct: 745 junction
peptides were called "toxic" on composition alone; every one of them had
a BLAST score of 0 AND a MERCI score of 0, i.e. zero actual toxin evidence.
Under the corrected rule 0 junctions are toxic and 574 are advisory
composition-flags only.
WHY IT MATTERED: "Toxic Junction (MHC)" was a hard rejection reason on this
construct, produced entirely by a heuristic the study had already documented
as unreliable for short peptides. The two phases were applying contradictory
toxicity standards to the same tool.
BACKED BY: Same basis as #1 (Sharma et al. 2022, the tool's own paper, which
shows the evidence-based hybrid outperforms the raw composition score). This
is an internal-consistency fix, not a new interpretive choice.

14. JUNCTION-LEVEL ALLERGEN CONSENSUS -- NOT AUTOMATED
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED/DISCLOSED: The paper requires AllerTOP + AllergenFP consensus on
EVERY junction peptide, with "a junctional peptide causing a positive
prediction by >= 2 predictors caused construct rejection" (Sec. II.A.a).
Full-construct-level consensus (both tools, whole sequence) IS run as
specified. Junction-level consensus is NOT automated.
WHY: AllerTOP and AllergenFP are manual, one-sequence-at-a-time web
submissions with no API. Our construct enumerates ~4,600 junction
peptides across all linker occurrences and lengths (MHC-I 8-11aa,
MHC-II 12-20aa) -- submitting each to two separate manual web tools is
not a feasible workload for a single construct, let alone every
candidate evaluated during iteration.
WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD: Junction-level Surface_Charge (>4, Sec. II.A.a's
short-peptide rule) and QN_Ratio (>0.30) are computed and reported per
junction as REVIEW flags -- these are cheap, local, and already
required by the paper for full constructs, so they're extended to
junctions as a proxy. The gap is printed explicitly at runtime (not
silently skipped) and is never used as a rejection trigger, since doing
so without real predictor results would be fabricating a result.
BACKED BY: Not backed by a source -- disclosed as a feasibility
limitation, the same category as #6's tool-length constraint. If
junction-level AllerTOP/AllergenFP consensus is required for the final
manuscript, it needs either a scripted browser-automation pass or
manual submission of the (much smaller) SET of unique junction
sequences after Phase 1G's redesign reduces junction count.

15. DEEPSOL <0.50 AS SOLE FAILURE -- CARRIED AS "REVIEW", NOT REJECTED
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Sec. II.A.b says a construct with DeepSol S2 < 0.50 is "predicted
insoluble" and is rejected. Where DeepSol is the SOLE failing criterion --
every other Sec. II.A check passing -- the construct is now carried forward
with status REVIEW instead of being rejected outright. The paper's compound
case (CamSol patch >8aa AND insoluble) remains a hard rejection, and DeepSol
failure alongside ANY other failure remains a hard rejection.
Controlled by DEEPSOL_ONLY_FAILURE_IS_REVIEW in Phase2A_secondaryScreening.py;
set it False to restore the paper's literal behaviour.

WHY: 12 constructs spanning the paper's own required scope -- seven target
antigens (Mpox L1R/B5R/A35R, HIV gp120/gp41/p24/p17) across all three
epitope classes -- were built and measured against DeepSol. Scores spanned
0.257-0.490 and NONE reached 0.50. The best (Vax_Final_a6aaa7d2, 0.4897)
misses by 0.0103; the FINAL construct (Vax_Final_0c83e66a, 484 aa) scores
0.4131.

NOTE THE FINAL CONSTRUCT SCORES LOWER THAN ITS PREDECESSOR, AND THAT IS NOT
A REGRESSION TO EXPLAIN AWAY -- it is the clearest single piece of evidence
that DeepSol is not steerable here. Vax_Final_0c83e66a is strictly MORE
compliant than Vax_Final_a6aaa7d2 on every rule the paper actually states:
it removes the Sec. I.G-violating epitope (ASIRYRQRLISLLSKK), eliminates all
3 junction-created linker-motif ambiguities, adds BigMHC immunogenicity
prioritization, and improves independently on MolProbity (0.64 vs 0.68),
clashscore (0.40 vs 0.52), Rama outliers (0.41% vs 0.61%, clearing 2D from
REVIEW to VALIDATED) and CamSol structural patch length (6 aa vs 11 aa).
Every measurable quality axis improved or held; DeepSol alone moved the
other way, by an amount (0.077) well inside the tool's own ~77% accuracy.
Solubility as reported by this tool is uncorrelated with construct quality
as defined by the rest of the paper's own criteria.

THIS IS A MEASURED TENSION BETWEEN TWO PARTS OF THE PAPER, NOT A GAP IN
EFFORT: five early prototype sequences (138 aa, a SUBSET of the seven-antigen
scope) scored 0.84-0.99 on the SAME tool. Every one of the 12 constructs that
actually satisfies Sec. I's full scope (seven antigens, three epitope
classes, the fixed AAY/GPGPG/KK linker set, the beta-defensin-3 adjuvant --
all specified elsewhere in the paper) scored 0.257-0.490. No sequence
feature distinguishes them from each other or predicts DeepSol
out-of-sample (n=11: GRAVY r=-0.33, net charge r=-0.05, hydrophobic fraction
r=-0.42 -- none significant), which is itself evidence that the ceiling is
set by what Sec. I requires being assembled at all, not by which epitopes
get picked within it. This is the same class of finding as deviation #12
(the adjuvant's native Cys count vs. the junction-Cys rule) -- two
requirements of the paper are in tension -- except here the tension is
empirically measured across 11 real constructs rather than read directly
off two sentences.

WHAT WAS TRIED (and failed) WITHIN the seven-antigen scope, confirming the
ceiling is structural and not a search failure:
  - GRAVY as a solubility proxy: predicted a low-GRAVY construct would score
    higher. It scored LOWER (0.393 vs 0.490). Falsified.
  - Net charge as a proxy: predicted a +10 construct would beat a +22 one.
    It scored LOWER (0.430 vs 0.490). Falsified.
  - Removing the beta-defensin-3 adjuvant to test whether the mandated
    adjuvant caused the insolubility: the adjuvant-free chain scored WORSE
    (0.300), i.e. the adjuvant IMPROVES predicted solubility by ~0.19.
  - A final batch of 4 further constructs spanning the feasible feature
    space: 0.408, 0.414, 0.444, 0.475. None reached 0.50.

SUPPORTING (composition, not the core argument): DeepSol was trained on
natural E. coli-expressed proteins. The construct is ~21% synthetic linker
by residue count (12x AAY, 10x GPGPG, 7x KK), giving 9.9% glycine and 7.9%
proline against natural averages of ~7% and ~5%. Nothing resembling ten
GPGPG repeats exists in its training distribution, and its reported accuracy
is ~77% -- a 0.0103 shortfall sits inside that error margin. "REVIEW" is the
paper's own vocabulary (Sec. II.C: "the final PASS/REVIEW/REJECT decision
with a short reason code"), so this uses an existing category rather than
inventing an exception.

BE HONEST ABOUT THIS ONE: this remains the deviation most likely to be
challenged, and it is not resolved -- it is documented as a real, measured
tension rather than asserted away. Do not present the construct as having
passed solubility screening. Report the FINAL construct's 0.4131 figure
explicitly wherever solubility is discussed (0.4897 was the superseded
Vax_Final_a6aaa7d2 -- do not quote the better number for the shipped
construct), and state plainly that no construct satisfying the paper's full
seven-antigen scope cleared 0.50.

16. RAPTORX SUBSTITUTED WITH NETSURFP-3.0 (server down)
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Sec. II.B specifies "PsiPred and RaptorX for the secondary
structure prediction." RaptorX was unreachable, so NetSurfP-3.0 (DTU) was
used as the second predictor instead. PsiPred is unchanged.
WHY: raptorx.uchicago.edu returned HTTP 500 on EVERY endpoint including its
root URL, leaking a Django debug traceback -- i.e. the whole application was
down, not one broken page. Verified directly on 2026-08-25:
    http://raptorx.uchicago.edu                        -> HTTP 500
    http://raptorx.uchicago.edu/StructurePropertyPred/ -> HTTP 500
    https://raptorx.uchicago.edu                       -> no response
WHY NETSURFP-3.0 SPECIFICALLY: of the live alternatives, it is the only one
that is both modern and genuinely INDEPENDENT of the PSIPRED lineage --
it is built on ESM-1b embeddings (2022). S4Pred was the convenient option
(same submission page as PSIPRED) but comes from the same group, so it
would have been a weak second opinion rather than an independent check.
JPred4 was the other serious candidate. SOPMA was rejected: server not
responding AND ~69% Q3, worse than PsiPred.
MEASURED RESULT: PsiPred 48.39% helix / 5.24% sheet / 46.37% coil;
NetSurfP-3.0 25.81% / 9.48% / 64.72%. Report BOTH -- the two methods
disagree on helix content by nearly 2x, and that disagreement is a real
finding about a largely-disordered construct, not something to average away.
BACKED BY: The substitution itself is forced by a third-party outage and is
disclosed on that basis. NetSurfP-3.0 is a real, citable, peer-reviewed
predictor. If RaptorX comes back before submission, re-running it would
remove this deviation entirely -- worth checking.

SUPPLEMENTARY (not a deviation, an addition): DSSP secondary structure is
also reported, assigned from the AlphaFold model's own coordinates via
pydssp. It is labelled clearly as an ASSIGNMENT from predicted coordinates,
not an independent prediction -- it inherits AlphaFold's errors and does NOT
count as the second predictor Sec. II.B requires. It is included because it
costs nothing and is self-consistent with the structure Steps 2C/2D use.
Measured: 17.34% helix / 14.92% sheet / 67.74% coil.

RELATED FINDING TO DISCLOSE (AlphaFold confidence): the Step 2B model has
mean per-residue pLDDT of 37.3 and pTM 0.16. Split by region: the
beta-defensin-3 adjuvant (res 1-45) averages pLDDT 93.6, while the epitope
chain (res 51+) averages 31.4. Only 46 of 496 residues (9.3%) reach the
pLDDT >= 70 threshold Sec. II.C uses to gate aggregation-patch calls. This
is expected, not an error: the epitope chain is a synthetic concatenation of
unrelated short peptides joined by deliberately flexible GPGPG/AAY/KK
linkers, so there is no defined global fold to predict. All three real
secondary-structure methods independently agree the construct is
coil-dominated (46-68%). Consequences: (a) most Aggrescan3D findings in the
epitope chain will be excluded by the pLDDT gate, so Sec. II.C's conjunctive
REJECT is unlikely to fire; (b) Phase III docks a low-confidence, largely
disordered model -- state this as a limitation.

17. JUNCTION-CREATED LINKER-MOTIF AMBIGUITY (extends Sec. I.G)
------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Sec. I.G screens epitopes for EMBEDDED AAY/GPGPG/KK motifs in their
own native sequence before assembly. Phase 1G now also rejects any AAY/
GPGPG/KK (or EAAAK) occurrence CREATED at a junction -- e.g. an epitope
ending "...LS" placed next to the KK linker can produce "...LSKK", read as
an extra, unintended KK site. Beta-defensin-3's own native C-terminus
("...RGRKCCRRKK") is exempted, the same shape as #12's junction-Cys
adjuvant exemption -- otherwise this rule could never be satisfied at all.
WHY: This is the identical rationale Sec. I.G states for the embedded-motif
screen ("preventing ambiguity in linker-boundary parsing") -- a
junction-created motif is exactly as ambiguous to a downstream parser as an
embedded one; the paper's own wording just didn't anticipate the case where
TWO compliant pieces of sequence combine to spell a linker.
MEASURED EFFECT: the pre-rebuild construct (Vax_Final_a6aaa7d2) carried 3
KK occurrences against 5 intended KK linker positions -- 2 of the 3 were
genuine junction ambiguity (one epitope's B-cell candidate ended in the
excluded-by-#1.G peptide ASIRYRQRLISLLSKK, directly adjacent to the KK
linker), the third sits inside the adjuvant's own native sequence and is
exempt. The rebuilt construct excludes that epitope at the pool level (see
below) and screens against this rule during assembly, reducing junction
ambiguity to the one adjuvant-internal, unavoidable case.
VERIFIED ON THE FINAL CONSTRUCT (Vax_Final_0c83e66a), independently of the
optimizer that produced it: 11 AAY occurrences vs 11 intended, 11 GPGPG vs
11 intended, 1 EAAAK vs 1 intended, and 5 KK vs 4 intended -- the single
extra KK being the adjuvant-internal "...RGRKCCRRKK", i.e. ZERO spurious
motifs outside the documented exemption.
BACKED BY: The paper's own stated rationale for the embedded-motif rule,
extended to a case it did not explicitly consider -- not a new citation.

18. HYDROPHOBIC SURFACE FRACTION IS A DISORDER FINDING, NOT A COMPOSITION FLAG
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING (not a rule change): Sec. II.C flags hydrophobic surface fraction >
0.25 as a structural concern. Measured directly against real solved
structures using the SAME FreeSASA code path Step 2C uses:
  RCSB TLR2 (6NIG, solved, soluble)              0.203
  RCSB TLR4 (8WTA, solved, soluble)               0.255
  Our construct, adjuvant region only (res 1-45,
    the one region with confident AlphaFold pLDDT 93.6)   0.274
  Our construct, epitope chain (res 46-496, pLDDT 31.4)   0.428
  Whole construct                                          0.416 (0.437 after
    the #II-1 residue-set correction below)
0.25 is a real, discriminating threshold on properly-folded protein -- both
TLR references and even our OWN well-folded adjuvant domain sit near it.
The flag is not a false positive from an arbitrary cutoff. But it tracks
model CONFIDENCE (pLDDT), not epitope COMPOSITION: the well-folded 45
residues of our own construct sit at 0.274, essentially matching the two
solved-structure controls, while the disordered synthetic-linker chain
sits at 0.428 -- an unfolded chain exposes residues a real fold would bury,
which is expected for a construct that is, by design, a concatenation of
short unrelated epitopes joined by deliberately flexible linkers with no
defined global fold (see the AlphaFold-confidence finding already on
record above). Re-selecting epitopes to chase a lower hydrophobic-surface
number would not address the actual cause and was not attempted.
CONFIRMED ON THE FINAL CONSTRUCT: the calibration table above was measured
on Vax_Final_a6aaa7d2; the rebuilt Vax_Final_0c83e66a reproduces it almost
exactly (hydrophobic surface fraction 0.402, mean pLDDT 34.8, only 45/484
residues = 9.3% at pLDDT >= 70). A materially different epitope selection
moved this number by ~0.01, which is the point: it is a property of the
construct's ARCHITECTURE (short epitopes on flexible linkers, no global
fold), not of which epitopes were chosen.
ALSO CORRECTED (Sec. II-1 in the remediation plan): Step 2C's
HYDROPHOBIC_RESIDUES set previously used {A,V,L,I,P,F,M,W,C} -- an
undocumented set inconsistent with the paper's own definition elsewhere
(Sec. I.E / II.A.a: {A,V,I,L,M,F,W,Y}). Aligned to the paper's set; this
raised the reported figure from 0.4163 to 0.4371 on the same structure --
reported explicitly rather than left as a silent discrepancy.

19. BigMHC "NEGATIVE SCORE" READ AS BELOW ITS OWN [0,1] MIDPOINT
------------------------------------------------------------------
CLARIFICATION: Sec. I.D says MHC-I candidates with "a negative [BigMHC]
score were deprioritized." BigMHC IM's actual CLI output (verified
directly: github.com/KarchinLab/bigmhc, 7-model ensemble, run locally on
CPU) is a bounded [0,1] sigmoid probability, not a raw pre-activation
score that can literally be negative. PRIORITIZED is therefore read as
score >= 0.5 (the tool's own neutral midpoint for a bounded probability)
and DEPRIORITIZED as < 0.5 -- the natural reading for a bounded output,
and disclosed explicitly rather than left as an unstated assumption.
Measured on the real MHC-I candidate pool (4,978 unique allele/peptide
pairs, all top-1%-percentile-rank survivors from Phase 1Db): 408
PRIORITIZED, 5,128 DEPRIORITIZED -- none excluded, per the paper's own
"not automatically excluded" wording.

20. SEMA 2.0 API IS ANTI-BOT BLOCKED -- MANUAL FALLBACK, NON-BLOCKING
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Sec. I.D specifies SEMA 2.0 for conformational B-cell-epitope
corroboration. SEMA 2.0 documents a real REST API (sema.airi.net/
openapi.json), which in principle needs no manual submission. In practice,
its actual prediction endpoint (POST /proteins) sits behind a commercial
anti-bot WAF (ServicePipe): verified directly this session with both the
`requests` library (form-encoded AND true multipart/form-data, matching
the documented schema) and a real headless Chromium via Playwright, which
was fingerprinted as automation and blocked outright (HTTP 403 on the bare
homepage). This is the same class of external blocker as RaptorX being
down (#16) or AllerTOP/AllergenFP having no API at all (#Manual-round) --
a third-party protection choice, not something to keep circumventing with
browser-automation evasion.
WHY NON-BLOCKING: Phase 1De attempts the automated call once per target,
falls back to a manual-submission template (paste the per-residue JSON

21. ALPHAFOLD FOLD CONFIDENCE GATES PHASE III SCOPE -- DOCK THE ADJUVANT,
    NOT THE WHOLE CONSTRUCT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Sec. II.D validates the 3D model with MolProbity, and Sec. III.A
then docks "the AlphaFold3 model of the vaccine" into TLR-2/TLR-4. Taken
literally that reads as: MolProbity passes, therefore dock the whole model.
We add a second, independent check between those two steps -- AlphaFold's
own confidence output -- and scope Phase III to what it supports.
WHY: MolProbity scores LOCAL STEREOCHEMISTRY (bond geometry, clashes,
rotamers, Ramachandran). It says nothing about whether the FOLD is right. A
random coil with clean geometry passes every MolProbity criterion. That is
not hypothetical here: this construct scores MolProbity 0.64 (EXCELLENT),
98.55% Ramachandran-favored, clashscore 0.40 -- a VALIDATED verdict -- while
AlphaFold reports the fold as almost entirely undetermined.
MEASURED (four independent axes, all agreeing; Step2B_AF3_Confidence_*.csv):
                             Adjuvant (1-45)   Epitope cassette (51-484)
  Mean pLDDT                     90.6              22.1 - 41.7
  Intra-region PAE                2.41 A           28.68 A (of ~31.75 max)
  Cross-model CA RMSD, 10 pairs   0.20 A           25.11 A (17.59-29.74)
  Residues at pLDDT >= 70          100%             0%
Whole model: pTM 0.16-0.17 across all five ranked models; 88.6% of residues
below pLDDT 50; the ONLY 45 residues reaching pLDDT >= 70 form one contiguous
run, 1-45 -- exactly the beta-defensin-3 adjuvant. Adjuvant<->cassette PAE is
30.10 A, i.e. AlphaFold cannot place the two relative to each other at all.
Docking the whole model would therefore dock ONE ARBITRARY CONFORMER of a
434-residue coil: swapping model_0 for any other ranked model moves that
region 17.59-29.74 A, so the docking result would not reproduce.
THIS IS ARCHITECTURE, NOT A MODELLING FAILURE. The construct is built on
flexible GPGPG/AAY/KK linkers precisely so each epitope stays independently
accessible to processing machinery -- a determined global fold is not the
design goal and would arguably be a defect. Deviation #18 reaches the same
conclusion from the hydrophobic-surface side (the 0.402 exposed-hydrophobic
fraction is a disorder signature, not an aggregation signature). No epitope
reselection changes this, and MD will not fold 434 de novo residues.
WHAT WE DO INSTEAD: Phase III docks residues 1-45. This is not a fallback --
it is the biologically correct interface. Human beta-defensin-3 IS the TLR-1/2
and TLR-4 agonist in this design; it is the reason the adjuvant is in the
construct at all. The epitope cassette has no proposed receptor interaction
to model. So the region AlphaFold determines confidently is exactly the
region the docking claim depends on.
THRESHOLDS ARE EXTERNAL CONVENTION, NOT FROM THE PAPER (stated in the tool
output itself, as with the Step 2D secondary QC flags): pLDDT >= 70 is
AlphaFold's own "confident" band and is already Step 2C's gate before it
trusts an aggregation patch; pTM >= 0.5 is the usual global-fold line;
cross-model RMSD <= 2.0 A is the conventional "same structure" agreement.
The manuscript specifies no fold-confidence criterion at all.
IMPLEMENTATION: Step 2B previously kept model_0 and discarded the other four
ranked models, all five summary_confidences_*.json and all five 484x484 PAE
matrices -- so none of the above was visible to anything downstream. It now
archives the full AlphaFold Server download under StepB/AlphaFold_Raw/<job>/
and reports the four axes. Step 2D prints TWO verdicts that cannot be
conflated: "FINAL DECISION (stereochemistry)" = VALIDATED, unchanged and
still methodology-defined, and "DOCKING READINESS (Phase III)" = DOMAIN_ONLY,
regions 1-45. Overall_Status logic was deliberately NOT modified: the paper
defines that verdict as stereochemistry, and quietly redefining it would
misrepresent the paper's own criterion.
WHY IT MATTERS FOR THE WRITE-UP: the common shortcut in this literature is to
dock the whole multi-epitope construct and report only pLDDT (or nothing).
A reviewer who asks for pTM, or who re-docks with a different ranked model,
breaks that result. Reporting the limit and scoping to the confident domain
is the defensible position, and costs nothing scientifically because the
adjuvant is the actual agonist.
CROSS-REFERENCES: #18 (same disorder conclusion, hydrophobic-surface route),
#16 (a third-party structural tool's limits reported rather than worked
around), #11 (Phenix refinement in 2C, so 2D validates the refined model).

22. IEDB EXPERIMENTAL CORROBORATION OF THE FINAL EPITOPE SET (NEW STEP 1H)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Added a step the manuscript does not specify. Every epitope in this
construct is PREDICTED -- MHCflurry, MHCnuggets and BepiPred-2.0 output,
filtered and ranked. Nothing in Sec. I ever asks whether any of them has been
observed in a laboratory. Phase 1H cross-references all 27 against IEDB's
curated assay records (query-api.iedb.org) and labels each one.
WHY: "our tools predicted this" and "our tools predicted this AND it is
independently documented with a PubMed citation" are very different claims,
and a reviewer will ask which applies. Being unable to answer is a weakness
that costs nothing to remove -- the check is free, changes no selection, and
the answer turned out to be favourable.
RESULT (27 construct epitopes):
   9  EXPERIMENTALLY CONFIRMED -- exact match, or contained within a curated
      epitope, with assay data attached
  12  OVERLAP a curated epitope by >=8 aa
   6  NOVEL -- no curated match
Two are EXACT matches to curated epitopes: SPRTLNAWV (HIV Gag; MHC-ligand

NOVEL IS NOT A DEFECT -- predicting epitopes with no prior curation is the

ORTHOPOXVIRUS SCOPE (disclose this): IEDB holds 29 Monkeypox-virus epitopes
but 17,903 vaccinia ones, and A35R/B5R/L1R have direct vaccinia orthologs --
orthopoxvirus cross-reactivity is the mechanism by which smallpox vaccination
protects against mpox. Restricting to MPXV alone would discard nearly the
whole relevant experimental record. Vaccinia matches are therefore included
but labelled CROSS_SPECIES (orthopoxvirus ortholog), never silently merged
with same-species evidence. Final split: 10 same-species, 11 cross-species.
THE CHECK THAT MAKES THIS TRUSTWORTHY: sequence matching alone is NOT
sufficient. A 9-mer is short and an orthopoxvirus proteome is ~200 kb, so a
curated epitope from an unrelated protein can share 9 residues by chance.
Protein NAMES cannot settle it either -- several genuine matches come back
labelled "Ankyrin repeat protein OPG189" or "Protein OPG161", unified
orthopoxvirus gene names bearing no resemblance to "B5R" or "A35R", so a
name-based filter would have rejected real orthologs. Instead every match is
verified against OUR OWN Phase 1A reference variants: does the curated
epitope actually occur in the antigen we sourced? All 21 matches align at
83-100% identity, so ZERO were rejected as coincidental. Had any fallen below
70% it would have been reported as NOVEL with the reason stated.
HOW TO WRITE IT UP: "21 of 27 epitopes (78%) have curated experimental
support in IEDB, 9 of them by exact or containment match with assay data."
Do NOT claim the novel 6 are validated, and do NOT drop the cross-species
qualifier on the 11 vaccinia-derived ones.

23. CUMULATIVE POPULATION COVERAGE REPORTED, NOT ONLY PER-EPITOPE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGED: Sec. I.F's >=90% criterion is applied PER EPITOPE, and that is how
Phase 1F reports it. Added a cumulative calculation over the epitope set that
is actually in the construct.
WHY: per-epitope coverage answers "what fraction of the population has an
allele binding THIS ONE peptide?" -- a legitimate prioritization signal, and
the right input to the >=90% gate. It is NOT the vaccine's coverage. A
vaccine delivers all its epitopes at once and a person is covered if ANY of
them binds one of their alleles, so the correct figure is computed over the
UNION of binding alleles, which is what IEDB's own Population Coverage tool
does for a multi-epitope set.
THE DIFFERENCE IS LARGE AND IT MATTERS: individually only 4 of 23
HLA-restricted epitopes clear 90%, which reads like a failure and is simply
the wrong comparison. Cumulatively:
  MHC-I   99.9949%  (union of 65 alleles across 12 epitopes)
  MHC-II  >99.99%   (union of 31 alleles across 11 epitopes)
  COMBINED >99.99%  (union of 96 of the panel's 107 alleles)
Same per-locus formula, same Hardy-Weinberg two-chromosome assumption, same
AFND frequencies as the per-epitope figure -- no second methodology.
REPORT THE PER-LOCUS BREAKDOWN, NOT JUST THE COMBINED NUMBER. The across-loci
formula multiplies complements, so three loci above ~85% force the combined
value over 99.9% almost regardless of the rest; quoted alone it is nearly
uninformative and looks like overreach. The honest layer is:
  MHC-I : A 57.10% (15/22 alleles) | B 98.96% (33/33) | C 98.86% (17/19)
  MHC-II: DPB1 85.56% (7/8) | DQB1 95.25% (6/7) | DRB1 99.54% (18/18)
HLA-A at 57.10% is the genuine weak spot of this construct and should be
stated plainly -- it is far more credible than a bare "100%" and it is the
number a reviewer would extract anyway. No locus saturates (no allele-
frequency sum reaches 1.0), so the figure is not a formula artifact; but the
tool deliberately prints ">99.99%" rather than "100.0000%", because across
six loci the complement product underflows to ~1e-9 and printing 100 would
assert that literally every person is covered.
B-cell epitopes are excluded -- they are not HLA-restricted, so population
coverage is undefined for them.

applies unchanged to every number above.
